A sewage backup is one of the most hazardous situations a property owner can face. Anyone who comes in contact with contaminated material is exposed to parasites, bacteria and viruses, among them giardia, E. coli, salmonella and hepatitis, all carried in raw sewage and all dangerous. Across Norman, Norman Restoration answers sewage emergencies 24/7, sending trained technicians who arrive with EPA-registered disinfectants and the right protective equipment so your property is decontaminated safely.

Water damage comes in categories, and sewage sits in the worst one: Category 3, called black water, the highest level of contamination and danger. Because of it, cleanup follows the toughest rules in the trade, which means full protective gear, barriers to contain the area, tearing out any porous material that met sewage and disinfecting all that is left. We work to the IICRC S500 standard that governs Category 3 losses, with the goal of returning your property to a condition safe to occupy.

Here is what the work covers: standing sewage is pumped out, contaminated water is extracted from the structure, porous materials the sewage touched (drywall, insulation, carpet and padding) are torn out and disposed of properly, hard surfaces are disinfected thoroughly, antimicrobials are applied, and the structure is dried so mold does not follow. To finish, thermal foggers and hydroxyl generators of industrial grade deodorize the space so the sewage smell does not linger.

How to Prevent Sewage Cleanup Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Do not wait on a slow drain, which can turn into a full blockage
  • Food waste, oils and grease block pipes, so put them in the trash and not the sink
  • A backwater valve at the sewer connection stops the city system from backing up into your home
  • Fit a strainer over each sink and shower drain so hair and debris stay out of the pipes
  • Clear tree roots near sewer lines, which can infiltrate and block the pipes
  • Have your sewer line inspected with a camera every 2 to 3 years to catch potential problems

Why Professional Sewage Cleanup Matters

No homeowner should try to clean up sewage personally. Because raw sewage is a biological hazard, the work demands trained people, protective equipment (gloves, boots, respirators and Tyvek suits) and disinfection products registered with the EPA and not sold to the public. Exposure to sewage pathogens can cause serious illness, including gastrointestinal disease, hepatitis, respiratory infections and skin infections.

Disposal matters as well, since everything the sewage contaminated has to be handled as biohazard waste. Simply placing sewage-contaminated drywall, carpet and insulation in your regular trash is both illegal and hazardous to sanitation workers and the community. Certified restoration companies maintain proper waste disposal protocols and relationships with licensed biohazard waste disposal facilities.

Warning Signs for Sewage Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Unexplained illness or gastrointestinal symptoms among household members
  • Drain openings or basement slabs coated with visible sewage residue
  • Strong sewage or rotten egg odors coming from drains or lower levels
  • Foul-smelling moisture showing up on flooring or along the walls
  • A cleanout that weeps sewage, or wet foul patches in the lawn along the sewer path
  • Carpet or flooring contaminated when a toilet overflowed with solid waste
  • Gurgling sounds from toilets or drains when other fixtures are used

Wall and Drywall Water Damage Help in Norman

Drywall acts like a wick. Paper and gypsum pull water up from the floor line, so the wet zone frequently ends a foot or more past the highest point the water itself touched. The painted face can look and feel normal while the back side and the insulation behind it are soaked. For that reason our team relies on moisture meter readings rather than appearance.

Not every wet wall has to be torn out. Firm panels wetted by clean water are frequently saved, in some cases with the baseboard taken off and small openings drilled so air can move inside the wall. If the drywall has gone soft, crumbles, shows mold or took in dirty water, we do a flood cut, meaning a level cut over the damaged area with all material beneath it removed.

Signs to Look For

  • A musty smell that is stronger near one wall or outlet
  • Speckled growth showing up where furniture sits against the wall or at corners
  • Baseboards that swell, warp or pull away from the wall
  • Discolored lower drywall with a visible line marking how high water wicked
  • One wall noticeably cooler under your hand than the rest
  • Bubbling, wrinkled or peeling paint and wallpaper low on the wall

What We Do

Our Wall and Drywall Water Damage Process

1

Find Every Wet Area

We scan each wall with a moisture meter and thermal camera and mark how high and how far the water traveled, including the opposite side and adjoining rooms.

2

Decide What Stays

You get a straight answer on what can be dried where it is and what must be removed, judged by the category of water, the time it sat and the firmness of the drywall.

3

Open Up or Air Out

For walls being saved, baseboards come off and air is directed into the cavity. On sections coming out, a straight flood cut is made and soaked insulation is removed.

4

Dry and Rebuild

Drying typically takes 3 to 5 days of air movement and dehumidification before the framing and surviving drywall meter dry. Then we insulate, hang and finish new drywall, reinstall trim and paint.

The Damage Timeline

The first few hours

Water Starts Climbing

Moisture enters where the panel meets the floor and wicks upward from there. Baseboards and the bottom plate take on moisture.

Hours 24 to 48

Wall Swells

Moisture keeps climbing, the baseboards puff up and blisters form in the paint. Mold can start on the paper facing inside the cavity.

Day 3 to 7

Panels Lose Strength

Paper peels from a core that is no longer firm. A musty odor grows stronger while the batts settle and mold covers more of the unseen face.

Past one week

Framing Is Affected

Mold and the start of decay show up on framing that has been wet the whole time. The amount of removal and reconstruction grows considerably.

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Electrical Hazards Low Down

Receptacles and cables sit low in the wall, right where water collects. Do not use outlets on a wet wall until the circuit has been checked.

Insulation Holding Water

Fiberglass batts soak up water and hold it against the studs. A wall with wet insulation will not dry from the room side alone.

Growth Behind the Paint

The back of the drywall is dark, still and lined with paper, which is ideal for mold. That side may be coated in growth even as the room side appears spotless.

Spread Into Adjoining Rooms

The neighboring room or closet gets wet as water travels below the wall plate. Our team inspects each wet wall from both sides, plus the floor running beside it.
